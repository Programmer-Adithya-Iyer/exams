# Communication Skills – U1

---

## Introduction
Communication is derived from the Latin word *commune-care* meaning “to share.”  
It is the process of transmitting information, ideas, attitudes, and emotions from one person to another.  
It is universal, independent of time, place, or context.  

**Definitions**  
- Ruesch (1961): Communication is a universal human function.  
- Balzer-Riley (2004): Communication is a reciprocal process of sending and receiving messages.  

**Importance**  
- Builds leadership: “He who communicates is he who leads.”  
- Ensures success: favorable responses and influence.  
- Facilitates decision-making and problem-solving.  
- Strengthens relationships through trust and clarity.  
- Motivates teams and resolves conflicts.  
- Bridges cultural differences in global contexts.  

---

## Communication Process
**Elements**  
- Sender: originator of message.  
- Message: idea to be conveyed.  
- Encoding: conversion into symbols.  
- Channel: medium of transmission.  
- Receiver: intended audience.  
- Decoding: interpretation of message.  
- Feedback: response ensuring understanding.  
- Noise: interference (physical, psychological, semantic).  

**Models**  
- Shannon-Weaver Model: linear, sender → channel → receiver → feedback.  
- Lasswell’s 5W Model: Who says? What? In which channel? To whom? With what effect?  

**Example**  
Classroom lecture: Teacher encodes knowledge into speech, transmits orally, students decode, feedback occurs through questions.  

---

## Verbal Communication
**Forms**  
- Oral: face-to-face, speeches, telephonic, video calls.  
- Written: letters, emails, reports, memos.  

**Advantages**  
- Quick feedback.  
- Personal touch.  
- Clarity through tone.  

**Disadvantages**  
- Risk of misinterpretation.  
- Less reflection time.  

**Case Study**  
- Vague email: “Do the needful” → confusion.  
- Clear email: “Submit the sales report by 5 PM today” → correct action.  

---

## Non-Verbal Communication
**Types**  
- Body Language: posture, gestures, facial expressions.  
- Paralanguage: tone, pitch, pauses, speed, volume.  
- Space Language (Proxemics): use of personal space.  
- Visuals: charts, diagrams, infographics.  

**Importance**  
Mehrabian’s Rule: 55% meaning from non-verbal cues, 38% from tone, 7% from words.  

**Examples**  
- Nodding = agreement.  
- Crossed arms = defensiveness.  
- Eye contact = confidence.  

---

## Barriers to Communication
**Definition**  
Barriers = conditions that interfere with effective exchange of ideas.  

**Types**  
- Physical/Environmental: noise, distance, faulty structure.  
- Physiological: poor eyesight, deafness.  
- Semantic/Language: jargon, ambiguity, misinterpretation.  
- Personal: age, education, interests.  
- Emotional: stress, bias, premature evaluation.  
- Socio-Psychological: status difference, closed mind, distrust.  
- Cross-Cultural: language, values, gestures, concept of time/space.  
- Organizational: hierarchy, filtering, poor communication policy.  

**Examples**  
- “Burn a CD” misunderstood by layman.  
- Traffic noise disrupting classroom lecture.  

**Overcoming Barriers**  
- Active listening.  
- Feedback.  
- Simplified language.  
- Climate of trust.  
- Awareness of cultural traditions.  
- Clear organizational structure.

## The 7 C’s of Effective Communication
| Principle     | Explanation | Example |
|---------------|-------------|---------|
| Clarity       | Clear thoughts, exact words | “Submit report by 5 PM” |
| Conciseness   | Least words, time-saving | “That man wanted water” |
| Completeness  | All facts included | Meeting agenda with time, venue |
| Courtesy      | Respectful, polite | “Could you please…” |
| Correctness   | No errors, precise facts | Grammar check, accurate data |
| Concreteness  | Supported with facts | “Sales increased by 20%” |
| Consideration | Audience-centered | Adjusting tone for executives vs students |

## Group Communication
**Meetings**  
- Formal: board meetings.  
- Informal: team huddles.  
- Purpose: decision-making, planning.  
- Roles: chairperson, participants, recorder.  

**Group Discussion (GD)**  
- Definition: structured conversation among 6–12 participants.  
- Difference from Debate: GD = collaboration, Debate = competition.  
- Types: factual, abstract, case-based, role-play, problem-solving, opinion-based, technical.  
- Process: introduction → initiation → discussion → consensus → conclusion.  
- Do’s: initiate, be relevant, listen, encourage, summarize.  
- Don’ts: interrupt, dominate, stay silent, go off-topic.  

**Skills Evaluated**  
- Communication, analytical thinking, team spirit, leadership, listening, knowledge, body language.  

**Case Study**  
GD on “Online vs Offline Education”: participants present pros and cons, moderator ensures consensus.

## Corporate Etiquette
**Workplace Etiquette**  
- Respect privacy.  
- Avoid gossip/politics.  
- Keep workstation clean.  
- Dress professionally.  
- Be punctual.  
- Respect colleagues.  
- Avoid foul language.  
- Handle confidential data responsibly.  

**Telephone Etiquette**  
- Pleasant voice.  
- Proper greetings.  
- Avoid odd hours.  
- Be concise.  
- Confirm recipient identity.  
- Don’t chew/eat while speaking.  
- End with polite closure.  

**Dialogue Example**  
Caller: “Good morning, may I speak to Mr. Sharma?”  
Receiver: “Good morning, this is Sharma speaking.”  
Caller: “I’m Harshul from XYZ Ltd. Is this a good time to talk?”  
Receiver: “Yes, please go ahead.”  
Caller: “I’d like to confirm tomorrow’s meeting at 11 AM.”  
Receiver: “Noted, thank you.”  
Caller: “Thank you, have a nice day.”

## Listening Skills
**Hearing vs Listening**  
- Hearing = passive, physiological.  
- Listening = active, involves attention, interpretation, response.  

**Types of Listening**  
- Comprehensive: understanding content.  
- Critical: evaluating arguments.  
- Empathetic: emotional support.  
- Appreciative: enjoyment.  

**Five-Step Process**  
1. Receiving.  
2. Understanding.  
3. Evaluating.  
4. Remembering.  
5. Responding.  

**Examples**  
- Lecture: comprehensive listening.  
- Debate: critical listening.  
- Counseling: empathetic listening.  

---

## Written Communication
**Paragraph Writing**  
- Topic sentence: central idea.  
- Unity: all sentences support topic.  
- Coherence: logical flow.  
- Adequate development: examples, facts.  

**Business Letters**  
- Structure: sender’s address → date → receiver’s address → salutation → body → closing → signature.  
- Types: sales letter, complaint letter, inquiry letter.  

**Example**  
Sales letter promoting new laptop model includes introduction, features, benefits, call to action, closing.

## Unit 2: Spoken and Written English

---

### Attributes of Spoken Communication
- **Spontaneity**: Spoken language is immediate, often unplanned, and influenced by context.
- **Tone and Intonation**: Meaning changes with pitch, stress, pauses, and rhythm.
- **Non-verbal Support**: Gestures, facial expressions, and body language reinforce spoken words.
- **Interaction**: Allows instant feedback, clarification, and adjustment.
- **Flexibility**: Speakers adapt vocabulary and grammar depending on audience.
- **Example**: A teacher explaining a concept adapts tone and pace based on student reactions.

### Attributes of Written Communication
- **Permanence**: Written words are recorded and can be referred back to.
- **Structure**: Requires organization, grammar, and coherence.
- **Formality**: More formal than speech; avoids slang and fillers.
- **Delayed Feedback**: Reader responds later, not instantly.
- **Precision**: Words must be chosen carefully to avoid ambiguity.
- **Example**: A business letter must follow format and clarity to avoid misinterpretation.

---

### Formal vs Informal Communication
| Aspect | Formal Communication | Informal Communication |
|--------|----------------------|------------------------|
| Nature | Official, structured, documented | Casual, spontaneous, unofficial |
| Language | Professional, precise | Everyday, colloquial |
| Channels | Prescribed (emails, reports, meetings) | Grapevine, chats, social media |
| Purpose | Goal-oriented, task-focused | Relationship-building, social bonding |
| Examples | Reports, presentations, official letters | Coffee-break talks, WhatsApp groups |

**Advantages of Formal Communication**  
- Clarity, accountability, compliance, professionalism, documentation.  

**Disadvantages of Formal Communication**  
- Time-consuming, impersonal, bureaucratic, inflexible.  

**Advantages of Informal Communication (Grapevine)**  
- Fast dissemination, rapport-building, innovation, morale boost.  

**Disadvantages of Informal Communication**  
- Rumors, misinterpretation, lack of accountability, secrecy issues.

### Variation in English (Indian, British, American)
**Spelling Differences**  
- BrE: Colour, Centre, Realise  
- AmE: Color, Center, Realize  
- IndE: Mostly BrE, but mixes (Colour, Centre, Timepass, Prepone).  

**Vocabulary Differences**  
- BrE: Lift, Flat, Post  
- AmE: Elevator, Apartment, Mail  
- IndE: Uses BrE words + Indianisms (Cousin-brother, Kindly do the needful).  

**Grammar/Usage**  
- BrE: “I have just eaten.”  
- AmE: “I just ate.”  
- IndE: “I will do a call to you.”  

**Pronunciation**  
- BrE: Non-rhotic (“Car” = /ka:/).  
- AmE: Rhotic (“Car” = /ka:r/).  
- IndE: Rhotic, syllable-timed rhythm.  

**Politeness/Style**  
- BrE: Polite, indirect (“Please take the necessary action.”).  
- AmE: Direct (“Please fix this.”).  
- IndE: Very formal (“Kindly revert back.”).

### Etiquette and Manners

#### Personal Behaviour
- Respect privacy, avoid gossip, maintain discipline.
- Dress appropriately, avoid strong perfumes or revealing clothes.
- Be punctual, keep workstation clean, avoid foul language.

#### Greetings and Introductions
- Use polite greetings: “Good morning,” “Good evening.”
- Introduce yourself clearly: name, role, purpose.
- Example: “Good morning, I am Harshul from XYZ Ltd., here to discuss the project.”

#### Telephone Etiquette
- Identify yourself immediately.
- Speak clearly, avoid eating/drinking while on call.
- Ask if it’s a good time before starting conversation.
- Leave clear messages: name, contact, purpose.
- Apologize for wrong numbers, don’t hang up abruptly.
- Example Dialogue:  
  Caller: “Good morning, may I speak to Mr. Sharma?”  
  Receiver: “Good morning, this is Sharma speaking.”  
  Caller: “I’d like to confirm tomorrow’s meeting at 11 AM.”  
  Receiver: “Noted, thank you.”  

#### Cell Phone Etiquette
- Keep phone on vibrate in meetings.  
- Don’t shout; move to quieter place.  
- Choose private spot for calls.  
- Use soft ringtones.  
- Keep business conversations confidential.

### Vocabulary Development

#### Dictionaries and Thesaurus
- **Dictionary**: Provides meanings, pronunciation, usage.  
- **Thesaurus**: Provides synonyms, antonyms, related words.  
- Example: “Happy” → synonyms: joyful, content, delighted.  

#### Words Often Confused
- Accept (receive) vs Except (exclude).  
- Advice (noun) vs Advise (verb).  
- Affect (influence) vs Effect (result).  
- Complement (complete) vs Compliment (praise).  
- Principal (head of school) vs Principle (law/truth).  
- Stationary (fixed) vs Stationery (writing paper).  
- Their (belonging) vs There (place) vs They’re (they are).  

#### One Word Substitutions
- A person who believes in God → Theist.  
- A person who compiles a dictionary → Lexicographer.  
- A tendency to favor relatives → Nepotism.  
- Impulsive stealing → Kleptomania.  
- Easily convinced → Gullible.  

#### Comprehension
- Involves reading, understanding, interpreting, and responding to text.  
- Skills: skimming (quick overview), scanning (specific info), intensive reading (detailed), critical reading (analysis).  
- Example: Passage on climate change → Questions on causes, effects, solutions.
